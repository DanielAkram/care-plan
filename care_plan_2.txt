====================================================
1. PROBLEM LIST / DRUG THERAPY PROBLEMS (DTPs)
====================================================

1. Generalized myasthenia gravis exacerbation (MGFA IIb) - progressive proximal weakness, ptosis, and intermittent diplopia over 2 weeks. Requires initiation of IVIG for rapid symptomatic control. (Needs additional/effective drug therapy)

2. IVIG infusion-related reaction risk - risk of headache, chills, fever, myalgia, and rate-related reactions. Requires premedication and infusion rate titration. (Adverse drug reaction - potential)

3. Thromboembolic risk with IVIG - additional risk factors include age, hypertension, and corticosteroid use. Requires hydration and rate management. (Safety monitoring)

4. Aseptic meningitis / headache risk with IVIG - more common at high total doses (2 g/kg) and rapid infusion. (Adverse drug reaction - potential)

5. Renal function / acute kidney injury risk with IVIG - lower risk with non-sucrose products and normal baseline renal function (eGFR >90), but requires monitoring and adequate hydration. (Safety monitoring)

6. Corticosteroid-related risks (prednisone 10 mg daily) - potential transient MG worsening at higher steroid doses, hyperglycemia, hypertension contribution, infection risk, GI irritation. Currently on omeprazole for GI protection. (Adverse effect monitoring / ongoing)

7. Volume/sodium load from IVIG and lisinopril interaction with BP - IVIG infusion and hydration may affect blood pressure; monitor in setting of controlled HTN. (Monitoring)

8. Adherence and understanding of complex infusion therapy - patient education needed regarding multi-day infusion schedule and warning symptoms. (Needs education)

====================================================
2. GOALS (SMART)
====================================================

1. Complete the full 5-day IVIG course (144 g total) as ordered without a treatment-limiting adverse reaction, within the scheduled 5-day window.

2. Achieve measurable improvement in MG symptoms (reduced ptosis, improved proximal strength, resolution of evening diplopia) within 1-2 weeks of completing IVIG, as assessed by neurology and/or MG-specific scoring.

3. Maintain respiratory stability with FVC at or above 2.8 L throughout treatment; no signs of respiratory compromise or myasthenic crisis.

4. Prevent serious IVIG-associated adverse events (thromboembolism, AKI, aseptic meningitis, severe infusion reaction) during and for 30 days after therapy.

5. Maintain blood pressure at goal (<130/80 mmHg) and stable renal function (SCr within 0.3 mg/dL of baseline 0.8 mg/dL) throughout and after the infusion course.

6. Patient will verbalize understanding of the infusion schedule, premedication plan, hydration instructions, and warning signs to report by the end of the first infusion visit.

3. PHARMACIST INTERVENTIONS / PLAN
====================================================

Product and dosing:
- Verify IVIG product selection; recommend a low-osmolality, sucrose-free, low-IgA product given thromboembolic/renal risk factors. Confirm final product with payer coverage.
- Confirm total dose 144 g (2 g/kg) divided over 5 days (approx. 28.8 g/day). Verify dosing weight with prescriber.
- Ensure product is at appropriate temperature and inspected prior to administration.

Infusion administration:
- Initiate infusion at a slow rate per product labeling and titrate upward as tolerated; use the slowest recommended maintenance rate given thromboembolic and headache risk.
- Ensure IV access and monitoring capability for each infusion visit.

Premedication (per protocol, confirm with prescriber):
- Acetaminophen 650 mg PO and diphenhydramine 25-50 mg PO 30-60 minutes prior to each infusion to reduce infusion-related reactions.
- For breakthrough headache, coordinate analgesia; if severe headache/neck stiffness occurs, hold and evaluate for aseptic meningitis.

Hydration and thromboembolism/renal protection:
- Ensure adequate oral hydration before and during each infusion day; consider pre-infusion IV hydration per protocol.
- Counsel on avoiding volume depletion; assess ambulation and thrombotic warning signs each visit.

Concurrent medication management:
- Continue pyridostigmine PRN, prednisone, lisinopril, and omeprazole. Reconcile all medications.
- Monitor for prednisone-related transient MG worsening and hyperglycemia; reinforce GI protection already provided by omeprazole.
- Hold or monitor lisinopril if hypotension or renal changes occur during infusion; coordinate with prescriber.
- Counsel patient to defer live vaccines for approximately 6 months after IVIG (reduced vaccine efficacy).

Patient education:
- Review 5-day schedule, expected symptom-improvement timeline (may take 1-2 weeks), and importance of completing full course.
- Educate on warning symptoms to report immediately: severe/persistent headache, neck stiffness, fever, chest pain, shortness of breath, leg swelling/pain, decreased urine output, dark urine, signs of worsening weakness or difficulty swallowing/breathing.
- Provide 24/7 pharmacy contact information.

Care coordination:
- Communicate care plan to Dr. Rodriguez / Neurology.
- Document baseline symptoms and coordinate post-treatment neurology follow-up.
- Verify insurance authorization and lot/product tracking for each infusion.

4. MONITORING PLAN & LAB SCHEDULE
====================================================

Baseline (prior to first infusion):
- SCr / eGFR (baseline SCr 0.8, eGFR >90 - documented)
- CBC with platelets (baseline Hgb 13.2, Plt 245 - documented)
- Serum IgA (documented within normal limits; no IgA deficiency)
- Baseline vital signs and blood pressure
- Baseline FVC (2.8 L documented) and MG symptom/strength assessment

During each infusion (Days 1-5):
- Vital signs (BP, HR, temp, RR) before infusion, with each rate increase, and at completion; monitor for infusion reactions.
- Assess for headache, flushing, chills, myalgia, chest/back discomfort.
- Monitor hydration status and blood pressure (HTN + IVIG volume considerations).
- Assess neuromuscular status and respiratory function; escalate/notify neurology if worsening weakness, dysphagia, or dyspnea.

Laboratory during/after course:
- Repeat SCr / eGFR mid-course and within 1 week after completion to detect IVIG-related AKI.
- Repeat CBC within 1-2 weeks after completion to assess for hemolysis (IVIG-associated hemolytic anemia) - watch for drop in Hgb.
- Consider urinalysis if renal function changes or signs of hemolysis (dark urine).

Post-treatment monitoring:
- Assess MG symptom response (ptosis, proximal strength, diplopia, stair climbing, arms overhead) at neurology follow-up within 1-2 weeks.
- Monitor for delayed adverse effects up to 30 days: thromboembolism (DVT/PE/stroke/MI), aseptic meningitis, delayed hemolysis, and renal dysfunction.
- Continue BP monitoring at goal <130/80 mmHg.
- Reassess FVC if any respiratory symptoms; ensure it remains at or above 2.8 L.

Follow-up:
- Pharmacist to follow up by phone within 24-48 hours after final infusion to assess tolerability and reinforce warning signs.
- Coordinate next IVIG cycle timing with neurology based on clinical response if maintenance therapy is planned.
